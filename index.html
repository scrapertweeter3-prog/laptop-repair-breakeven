A $420 repair on a $440 laptop

Repair quotes arrive without a denominator. The laptop cost $1,100 new and is three years into a five-year working life, so the years left in it are worth $440 on a straight line. A $420 screen and board quote spends 95 percent of that remaining value on one repair. The bill feels like half a laptop; against the machine's remaining life it is nearly all of it.

Price the repair like a purchase. $420 for two more years is $210 a year, and the $1,100 replacement across five years is $220 a year, so the repair wins by $10 a year as long as nothing else fails. One more $200 board fault in year four turns the repair spend into $620 over the same stretch, $310 a year, which is 41 percent worse than buying new. The resale angle rescues nothing either: a repaired three-year-old brings around $250 and an unrepaired one around $80, so at most $170 of the $420 ever comes back.

Replacement hides a second invoice. Moving files, reinstalling tools and re-pairing devices eats six hours, and six freelance hours at $25 is $150, which turns the $1,100 new machine into $1,250 and $250 a year across five. Priced that way the repair beats new by $40 a year, right up until the second fault. The rule that survives both ledgers: repair when the quote sits under half the remaining-life value. A $220 fix is exactly half of $440 and sits on the line. The $420 quote is 95 percent and fails.
